## What tracing shows

All 300/300 prefix checks pass: 20 native tool proposals/executions, 50 leaf arguments, and
30 policy-selected sink fields. Saved online evidence and DCPG agree with local prefix replay,
and every executed proposal has its receipt before native runtime entry.

On the same 30 source-field pairs, the exact baseline has 10 candidates; LCS and the saved
ordered cascade each have 30. Every cascade pair first hits Tier2. Tier1 is disabled in this
passive condition; Tier3 and Tier4 are skipped after the earlier hit. Zero auditor requests
reflect trigger ineligibility, not a negative causal judgment. These are candidate/routing
counts, not accuracy measurements.

A concrete limitation appears in all 20 participant-email fields. Their full addresses were
provided directly in the user prompt and do not occur as complete literals in the eligible
calendar output, yet whole-output LCS scores range from 0.8696 to 0.9688 and trigger Tier2.
These matches cannot establish that the model obtained the addresses from the tool output.
This is evidence for examining lexical candidate specificity in this setting; it is not an
independent false-positive rate or a confirmed flaw in the original authors' implementation.

The optional decoded-scalar diagnostic passes 12/12 frozen controls and scores all 330 scalar
comparisons across the 30 selected fields. None is unavailable. Among 15 comparisons with an
assigned payload region, no complete target literal occurs in that region. The five event-ID
comparisons align no payload characters. The ten email comparisons require some payload
characters in every optimal LCS alignment: 4–5 characters for John Doe's address and 6–9 for
Maria Garcia's address, against scalar LCS lengths 14 and 13 respectively. These scattered
character alignments do not establish copied instructions, maliciousness or model reliance.
